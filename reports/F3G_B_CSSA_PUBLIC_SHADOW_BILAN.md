## What public reality already teaches us

### 1. Public truth is not automatically coherent

Three separate conflict groups appeared immediately:

- Manager Général current-state publication mismatch;
- one source exposing two kickoff times for Bogny — Sedan;
- two sources exposing different kickoff times for Rethel — Sedan 2.

That validates the need for provenance/freshness/coherence before action.

### 2. Corroboration matters too

Two secondary observations agreeing on Sedan — Uckange remain one corroborated result rather than becoming a false contradiction.

The shadow layer therefore distinguishes disagreement from independent agreement.

### 3. The club really exposes multi-team operational density

Recent public Association output shows many team/plateau/referee events on the same weekend.

This anchors our synthetic whole-club load in observable reality without claiming we know the internal workload behind each event.

### 4. Two F3G-A blind spots are partly reduced

Public evidence confirms the existence of:

- technical-direction / academy coordination;
- an active matchday volunteer program.

But their capacities remain unknown.

So we improved ontology/workflow coverage, not capacity calibration.

## Next useful step

Build a public-anchored operating envelope:

- keep hard public facts as facts;
- keep unknowns unknown;
- assign explicit ESTIMATED ranges only where simulation needs a number;
- run low/base/high envelopes;
- make every estimate replaceable by later field evidence.

That lets the project keep moving now and makes later real calibration a parameter replacement rather than a redesign.
